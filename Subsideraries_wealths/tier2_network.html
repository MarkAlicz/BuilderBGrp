Tier-2 interweave — where the roster actually overlaps

Primary affiliations put 128 people in 128 chairs. Second-degree ties — prior offices, board seats, ownership, shared newsrooms and lecture halls, one marriage — put many of them in the same room twice. Institutions are squares, people are circles, and an institution touched by two or more participants is a bridge.

Provenance. Tier-1 (person to current organisation) is verbatim from your document. Tier-2 ties, ownership chains and financial figures are from my own knowledge, cutoff mid-2026, graded A (settled public record), B (well documented, re-check against a current filing) or C (indicative order of magnitude only). Market caps and private valuations move fast; treat them as scale, not price.

On personal wealth. A figure appears only where one is actually published or directly derivable from a disclosed stake or transaction. That covers 20 of the 128. For the rest — ministers, editors, academics, and serving intelligence and military officers — no credible figure exists, and rather than generate a plausible-looking number attached to a real person's name, the field is left empty and the reason is stated on the node. Controlled institutional mass is given instead, and is the more useful quantity here in any case.

What the graph is and isn't. An edge means a shared institution, not a shared intention. Two people on the same alumni node have a documented path between them and nothing more. Betweenness is computed with Brandes' algorithm over the filtered graph and identifies structural brokers — nodes that many shortest paths must traverse — which is a property of this dataset's boundary, not of the world. Adding one unrecorded board seat can reorder the top ten. Treat the ranking as a prompt for where to look next, not a finding.
